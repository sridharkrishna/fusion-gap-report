    How to read the scores

    The same ladder applies every month, so a score means the same thing in July as in December. Technical gaps (net gain to heat conversion) use the left column; institutional gaps (investment, regulation, supply chain, workforce) use the right. The last column shows which gaps sit on each rung in the selected issue.

    A score moves only on a new physical demonstration or an institutional decision with legal effect. Announcements, simulations, design papers and single funding rounds don't move scores. Each gap's “what would move it up a point” line appears when you pick it above.

    What moves Private Investment

    Funding rounds alone don't count, so two things can move this axis: (1) the FIA's annual cumulative total, published each July, crossing into a new band; (2) capital with legal effect, such as a plant's financial close, a binding power purchase agreement, or an enacted public appropriation.

      Glossary of terms and acronyms

      Method: headline scores rate world-system readiness (0 = gap wide open, 10 = solved for commercial deployment), continuing the 2020 baseline analysis. Country scores rate national standing. Standing rule (Sept 2026): state-level fusion licences do not move Regulatory Clarity; revisit on an NRC final rule or a federal-level licence. “Europe” = EU + UK. The prediction band combines five methods: industry self-report (FIA survey), crowd forecasting (Metaculus), independent technical review, historical base rates (ITER slippage), and the weakest-link logic of this report's own gap scores. Scores are analyst judgement grounded in cited milestones, not an official index.
